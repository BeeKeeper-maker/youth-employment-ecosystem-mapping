## Objective 3:  Youth Employment Ecosystem Mapping


The youth employment ecosystem across the three upazilas comprises a layered set of actors spanning government training and social protection offices, NGO and development partners, private employers, and the SCI implementing partner, with significant variation in depth and inclusivity across upazilas. In Satkhira Sadar, the ecosystem is the most developed: TTC Satkhira operates a government TVET centre with a dedicated job placement cell, employer links to RFL and Walton, and job fairs; DYD provides vocational training, microcredit, and employer referral; DWA offers multi-trade training alongside GBV referral and legal support; BSCIC provides 5% interest loans and plot allocation with a 30% women's priority quota; and Rishilpi International Foundation delivers home-based production linkage with raw material delivery and product collection, making it among the most inclusive actors for women with mobility and disability constraints.

In Debhata, Uttaran Shimanta Technical Institute stands out as the benchmark actor across all three upazilas, combining free residential training, transport allowance, job counselling, job fairs, six-month follow-up, and job replacement support; Taskia Garments offers women-only tailoring and block-batik apprenticeships with seasonal employer demand; and Noabeki Gonomukhi Foundation provides free training, loan support, and livelihood linkage, though its reported placement figures require independent verification.

In Ashashuni, where the ecosystem is most constrained, the most relevant NOW-tier actors are local tailoring shops and Tohomina Ladies Garments, which offer women-only apprenticeships aligned sector preference, SUS through its guru-shishya apprenticeship model with loan support, and local poultry and livestock providers with 80 percent female participation.

Across all three upazilas, Breaking the Silence as SCI's implementing partner is consistently identified by FGD participants as the most trusted source of employment information, representing the highest-potential strategic asset for programme engagement. The most inclusive actors those offering home-based work, PWD accommodation, minority-sensitive practice, and complaint mechanisms are Rishilpi and Uttaran; actors requiring facility upgrades before endorsement include Taskia Garments, Satata Ladies Tailors, and Monayem Computer and Training Centre. Existing partnership and entrepreneurship opportunities include BSCIC trade fairs, DYD and TTC job fairs, Rupantor's internship and development-sector referrals, Progress Organisation's post-training support, and the Satkhira Chamber of Commerce as an entry point to the private sector employer network. The highest-potential actors for immediate strategic engagement classified as NOW across all three upazilas are TTC, DYD, DWA, BSCIC, Rishilpi, and Breaking The Silence in Sadar; Uttaran, Taskia Garments, Noabeki, DYD, DWA, and Breaking The Silence in Debhata; and local tailoring providers, SUS, local livestock providers, and Breaking The Silence in Ashashuni.
